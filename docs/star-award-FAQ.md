FAQ
Find answers to the most frequently asked questions about the transition from the Call Out recognition programme to the new Star Award. This section addresses key queries on deadlines, nomination processes, and what to expect from the new programme launch.

What is the Star Award Recognition Program?
The new Star Award programme will offer a simpler, more transparent, and engaging way to recognise great work. It aims to bring greater visibility and fairness to recognition across all teams. 

Who is eligible to give and receive recognition?
All active full-time and part-time employees can participate in the recognition programme by giving or receiving awards for outstanding contributions. Contractors are not eligible, in line with government guidelines. The programme is open to all teams, roles, and locations, maintaining a consistent and inclusive approach for eligible staff.

What are the recognition tiers and their frequency?
The recognition program features three distinct award tiers, each with its own nomination and selection process. 
The Star Award is led by employee nominations and distributed quarterly, highlighting individuals recognized by their colleagues for outstanding contributions. 
Each quarter, leaders review the Star Award recipients and choose 36 team members whose performance qualifies them for the All-Star Award . All Star winners will be celebrated during their practices’ All Hands.
At the highest level, the Global All-Star Award is determined annually by employee votes and celebrates exceptional individuals whose impact resonates across the organization.
 

What is the value of the awards?
The Star Award will provide gift cards, aiming to deliver greater value than the Call Out store front, offering you more flexibility in how you choose to spend your reward. Values may vary by location depending on the cost of living. You can view all corresponding figures under Recognition Value Equivalences. 

What happens to unused stars, and what are their implications?
You can nominate one Star per quarter, regardless of whether you submitted a nomination in previous quarters. Stars operate on a use-it-or-lose-it basis to encourage timely recognition and prevent stockpiling. This ensures recognition remains meaningful, supports regular budget planning, and keeps the programme fair and sustainable. 

Do stars carry over leaves (maternity, paternity, sick, etc.)? 
Stars do not carry over during any type of leave. This encourages timely recognition rather than stockpiling nominations. Employees on leave can still receive Stars as usual; however, the ability to give a Star does not carry over.

When do Star Awards nominations need to be submitted? 
The ability to award Stars will automatically expire on the last day of each quarter. 

How do I submit a nomination?
Employees should submit nominations via the designated platform with clear, detailed justification that highlights accomplishments and alignment with Version 1 Core Values. Approved nominations trigger an email to the recipient and recognition on Viva Engage; if not approved, constructive feedback is given for improvement.

What are the use rules?
Each employee may give one Star Award per quarter to ensure fair recognition. Self-nominations are not allowed; only colleagues can nominate based on exceptional contributions. Focus nominations on outstanding achievements, not routine duties. Do not nominate the same employee for the same reason in consecutive quarters. Avoid bias, reciprocal nominations, and last-minute submissions to maintain the integrity of the program. Use the “Praise” feature for daily positive interactions.

What categories should nominations focus on ?
It is suggested that nominations explain how the nominee stands out in one of these categories:
• Collaboration & Engagement: Promoting teamwork, building partnerships, and engaging colleagues.
• Customer Impact: Delivering value to customers or improving satisfaction.
• Innovation & Growth: Introducing new ideas or driving process improvements.
• Performance & Efficiency: Boosting productivity, streamlining workflows, or achieving results.
• Quality & Compliance: Maintaining high standards and meeting compliance requirements.

What are examples of impactful recognition?
• Leading a cross-functional task force to resolve a critical client issue.
• Developing an onboarding checklist that reduced ramp-up time by 30%.
• Receiving unsolicited client praise for exceptional service.
• Volunteering for community initiatives reflecting our Core Values.

What are grounds for rejecting a nomination?
• Nominations need to reflect and uphold the Core Values of Version 1. Any nomination that does not demonstrate clear alignment with these values may be rejected .
• Nominations based solely on standard tasks or those lacking measurable impact will not be considered.
• Ethical standards must be maintained in the nomination process. Examples of breaches include self-nomination and submitting multiple Star nominations for the same individual.
• Each nomination should provide clear and compelling justification. Submissions lacking adequate evidence or rationale for recognition will be rejected.
• Nominations should be free from personal bias or favouritism. Any submission that appears to be influenced by personal relationships or unfair preferences will not be accepted.
• Individuals who have engaged in inappropriate behaviour or who do not meet eligibility criteria are not suitable for nomination.
Should a nomination be rejected, you will be informed and given the opportunity to submit another Star Award nomination, provided it is done by the end of the quarter.

How are All-Star winners selected?
All eligible Star Award nominations are subject to a thorough review. Each nomination is evaluated by a central Recognition Team, against established criteria, including:
• What: The specific actions or achievements for which the individual is being recognised.
• How: The way these actions were carried out, reflecting core values and behaviours.
• Impact: The measurable effect or change resulting from the nominee’s contributions.
• Reach: The scope of influence, including how many people or areas were positively affected.
• Sustainability: The lasting or ongoing benefits of the nominee’s actions.
• Evidence: Supporting documentation or examples provided to substantiate the nomination.
Nominations are scored using a standard rubric on a scale from 1 to 5 for each criterion. This approachco helps ensure that all submissions are evaluated impartially and in line with programme guidelines.

What are manager responsibilities?
Managers play a vital role in fostering a positive recognition culture within their teams and their responsibilities include:
• Actively recognising team members: Managers should consistently acknowledge the achievements and efforts of their team members, ensuring that recognition is timely and meaningful.
• Ensuring less visible contributors are not overlooked: It is important for managers to pay attention to all team members, including those whose contributions may not be immediately apparent, so that everyone receives fair recognition.
• Monitoring patterns to avoid bias: Managers are expected to observe recognition trends and take steps to prevent any unconscious bias, ensuring that awards and acknowledgments are distributed equitably.
• Celebrating awards in meetings and onboarding new joiners into the recognition culture: Managers should highlight award recipients during team meetings and actively introduce new employees to the organisation’s culture of recognition, helping them feel valued and included from the outset.
