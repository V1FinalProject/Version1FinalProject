## **Guidelines for the Star Award Recognition Program**

The S**tar Award Recognition Program** is a meaningful and strategic initiative designed to reinforce Version 1’s values, celebrate impactful contributions, and nurture a culture of appreciation across the organisation. It is not a token gesture, but a deliberate investment in recognising excellence and fostering a strong sense of belonging. 

Through consistent and thoughtful recognition, the program aims to highlight the behaviours and achievements that exemplify our values, strengthening our shared commitment to a culture of appreciation and excellence.

#### **1\. Requirements**


Each Star Award nomination must include: 

-    WHAT impact your nominee has made.
    
-    HOW they demonstrated Version 1 values.
    

#### **2\. Eligibility & Access** 


-    All active full- and part-time employees can give and receive recognition.
    
-    Contractors can't give or receive awards due to government guidelines. 
    
-    Open across all teams, roles, and locations.
    

#### **3\. Submission & Approval Process** 



-    Submit nominations via the platform with clear justification. 
    
-    Approvals are routed to the Recognition Team for review. 
    
-    Approved nominations are emailed to the recipient and may be posted to Viva Engage. 
    
-    Employees whose nominations are not approved will receive feedback to help understand the decision and improve future submissions.
    

#### **4\. Ethical Use Criteria** 

-    Each employee can give only ONE Star per quarter. 
    
-    No self-nominations. 
    
-    Focus on impact, behaviour and values, not routine tasks. 
    
-    Do not nominate the same employee for the same reason in consecutive quarters. 
    
-    Star Award is for exceptional contributions only.
    
-    Use “Praise” for day-to-day recognition.
    
-    Avoid bias, reciprocity, or last-minute submissions.
    

#### **5\. Manager Responsibilities**


-    Actively participate in recognising team members. 
    
-    Proactively ensure that less visible contributors are not overlooked.
    
-    Monitor recognition patterns to avoid bias or negligence. 
    
-    Encourage recognition and celebrate milestones. 
    
-    Celebrate awards and praise in regular meetings.
    
-    Ensure new joiners are onboarded into the recognition culture.
    
-    Weekly summaries or “Wall of Fame” features are encouraged.    
    

#### **6\. Feedback & Continuous Improvement** 


This scheme will be reviewed quarterly to ensure it remains aligned with employee feedback and evolving recognition needs.  

#### **7\. Examples of Impactful Recognition** 


*“Leaded a cross-functional task force that resolved a critical client issue within 48 hours, involving Finance, IT, and Customer Success. The solution prevented a potential contract loss and improved client satisfaction scores.”*

*“Developed a new onboarding checklist that reduced average ramp-up time for new hires by 30%. This was adopted across three departments and praised by HR for its clarity and effectiveness.”* 

*"Received unsolicited praise from a client for going above and beyond during a product rollout. The client specifically mentioned their professionalism, responsiveness, and clarity.”* 

*"Volunteered with Community First, coordinating donations. Their commitment reflected Version 1’s values and strengthened our community impact.”*

#### **8\. Grounds for Rejecting a Star Award Nomination** 


| Not aligned with Version 1 values | Nomination doesn’t demonstrate behaviours tied to company values. |
| --- | --- |
| Lack of impact/ Routine task | Recognition is requested for standard duties rather than exceptional contributions. |
| Breach of ethical use | Self-nomination. Recognition sought for trivial or personal reasons. |
| Insufficient justification | Nomination lacks detail about what was done and how values were demonstrated. |
| Bias/Favouritism | Evidence of “visibility bias” (e.g., only high-visibility roles being nominated). Repeated nominations between the same individuals suggesting reciprocity. |
| Inappropriate behaviour | Recognition for actions that contradict company policies, code of conduct, or inclusivity standards. |
| Non-eligibility | Contractors being nominated to receive or give awards. |
| Budget/Integrity risks | Large numbers of last-minute nominations can complicate the review process, as can reciprocal nominations or attempts to exaggerate impact. |

Should a nomination be rejected, you will be informed and given the opportunity to submit another Star Award nomination, provided it is done by the end of the quarter.


#### **1\. Eligibility**


-    All active full- and part-time employees who received at least one Star Award during the quarter are eligible for an All-Star Award.
    
-    Contractors can't give or receive awards due to government guidelines. 
    
-    Open across all teams, roles, and locations.
    

#### **2\. Process** 


1.  Review all eligible Star Award nominations for the period. 
    
2.  Score each nomination against the criteria using the scoring rubric. 
    
3.  Discuss as a panel to ensure consistency and challenge bias. 
    
4.  Shortlist the top-scoring nominations for All-Star Awards (quarterly). 
    
5.  From quarterly All-Star winners, further shortlist for Global All-Star (annual) with emphasis on sustained or organisation-wide impact. 
    

#### **3\. Responsibilities**


-    Ensure all decisions are evidence-based and documented. 
    
-    Monitor for bias (e.g., favouring visible roles or locations). 
    
-    Provide constructive feedback to unsuccessful nominees where appropriate. 
    
-    Celebrate and communicate winners in line with the communication plan. 
    

#### 4. **Shortlisting Criteria** 


-    What: What did the individual do? Was it above and beyond their normal role? 
    
-    How: How did they demonstrate Version 1’s core values? 
    
-    Impact: What was the measurable or observable impact? 
    
-    Reach: Did the contribution benefit a team, department, the whole company, or the wider community? 
    
-    Sustainability: Is the impact likely to last or inspire others? (Is this a behaviour we are trying to drive?) 
    
-    Feedback/Evidence: Is there supporting evidence such as client praise, peer testimonials, or data? 
    

#### **5\. Scoring Rubric for Shortlisting Panel** 


Each criterion is scored from 1 to 5, with descriptors provided below:   
 

| Criteria | Score 1 | Score 2 | Score 3 | Score 4 | Score 5 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| What | Exceeds expectations | Slightly exceeds expectations | Clearly exceeds expectations | Significantly exceeds expectations | Exceptional contribution that redefines expectations |
| How | Limited demonstration of values | Clear demonstration of values | Consistent alignment with multiple values | Strong alignment with multiple values, influencing others | Outstanding embodiment of values, inspiring others |
| Impact | Limited impact | Noticeable impact | Meaningful impact | High impact | Transformational impact |
| Reach | Individual only | Small team | Department or Function | Organisation-wide | External/Community impact |
| Sustainability | Short-term effect | Limited effect | Moderate effect | Long-lasting effect | Enduring and scalable impact |
| Feedback/ Evidence | Informal feedback available | Anecdotal feedback from one or more sources | Documented supporting feedback or measurable outcomes | Strong evidence from multiple internal sources | Strong evidence from both internal and external sources |
